Lessons: all three learning levels

Choose Foundations, Build Your Skills, or Challenge for your experience and current question. Read independently; bring new techniques, partner work, and blade work to your coach with the appropriate protective equipment.

Your Place on the Strip

Your questions. Your decisions. Your next discovery.

What kind of fencer are you becoming?

You belong on the strip, and your questions belong in the conversation. Saber asks you to move, notice, and make decisions. Choose a mission that stretches your thinking, then bring your discoveries to your coach.

Key ideas

  • Choose a level by experience, not age.
  • Make one prediction before seeing the answer.
  • A good question is progress.

Your mission: Choose a topic and one question you want to explore for yourself.

Foundations

Start small

Read the preview, look at its picture, and try the question. If a word is new, open the glossary. You can return to any lesson.

Connect to class

Think of one moment from your last lesson. Which topic might explain it? Your coach can connect these ideas to the technique you are learning.

Build Your Skills

Use the learning loop

Observe, predict, check, explain. When your prediction changes, name the new detail that changed your mind.

Choose a useful goal

Try 'notice when the attack finishes' instead of 'win every touch.' A useful goal describes something you can watch or do. Find a woman fencer in the video collection whose choices make you curious. Notice a decision you would like to understand.

Challenge

Explain both choices

When you choose an action, explain one reason for it and one situation where another action might work better.

Be an honest detective

Separate what you saw from what you guessed. 'The fencer retreated' is an observation. 'They wanted a parry' is a possible explanation.

Ask your coach: Which one topic would help me most this week?

Useful words

Bout 一场比赛
A fencing contest between two fencers.
Mission 学习目标
One clear thing you want to notice or learn.

Think, then check

Which is the clearest learning mission?

  1. Never lose again.
  2. Be faster than everybody.
  3. Identify one moment when an attack falls short.
  4. Finish every lesson today.

Answer: Identify one moment when an attack falls short.

A small, observable goal lets you check what you learned and ask a useful follow-up question.

Ready, Safe, and Respectful

Good fencing starts before the first touch.

What should you check before saying 'ready'?

Equipment, space, and attention help everyone enjoy fencing. Reading and watching are independent activities. Any partner blade exercise belongs in a properly supervised practice with appropriate protective equipment.

Key ideas

  • Check space and equipment with an adult or coach.
  • Respond to the referee's commands.
  • Respect your opponent, the officials, and yourself.

Your mission: Name one space check, one equipment check, and one respectful habit.

Foundations

Make room

For familiar weapon-free footwork, use a clear, non-slip space approved by an adult. Keep away from stairs, furniture, people, and pets.

Check, then begin

Ask your coach to show you how to check your kit. Tell an adult about damaged equipment. Do not improvise a weapon or fence an unprotected person.

Build Your Skills

Listen all the way through

Be ready and still before the start command. On 'Halt,' stop and do not begin another action. Listen for the referee's explanation.

Be a helpful partner

Agree on the coach's task and speed before a supervised drill. Take turns honestly. If your partner is not ready, wait.

Challenge

Notice your surroundings

Before an activity, ask: Is the space clear? Is the equipment ready? Is the right supervision present? If something changes, pause and check again.

Handle problems calmly

If your mask feels loose, something hurts, or equipment seems faulty, alert the coach or referee. Solving the problem comes before finishing the task.

Ask your coach: Which activities may I do independently, and which need you or another adult?

Useful words

Halt 停
The command that stops the fencing exchange.
Salute 敬礼
The formal gesture of respect used in fencing.

Think, then check

Your practice partner has removed their mask. What should you do?

  1. Try one gentle touch.
  2. Keep your blade activity stopped and wait for proper readiness.
  3. Practice only head cuts.
  4. Ask them to close their eyes.

Answer: Keep your blade activity stopped and wait for proper readiness.

Never start blade activity against an unprotected partner. Resume only with proper equipment, readiness, and supervision.

Ask your coach to demonstrate your own equipment checks. No dedicated equipment-check video has been verified in this collection.

The Strip, Targets, and Lights

Read the playing space and the scoring machine.

Two lights are on. Do both fencers score?

The strip is your playing space. The scoring machine records electrical signals, while the referee decides whether a touch counts and who receives it. Learn to read both the space and the exchange.

Key ideas

  • Know where you are on the strip.
  • Saber target includes head, torso, and arms; not hands or legs.
  • Two lights require the referee to judge the exchange.

Your mission: Point to the start lines, rear limits, and valid target areas.

Foundations

Find the landmarks

Locate the center, on-guard lines, side boundaries, and rear limits. Watch how much room a retreating fencer still has.

Read a light carefully

A light is information, not the whole decision. Even one registered touch may be annulled for a rule issue. Listen to the referee.

Build Your Skills

Follow both fencers

Before looking at the lights, describe the actions. Did an attack arrive? Was it parried or avoided? This helps you understand the decision.

Protect your space

Retreating can create distance, but the strip has an end. Include the remaining space when choosing your next action.

Challenge

Do not borrow another weapon's rules

Foil, epee, and saber differ. A white off-target light in foil or an epee double touch does not explain saber scoring.

Investigate no light

No light alone cannot tell you why a touch failed. It might have missed, landed off target, or involved equipment. Let the referee handle equipment checks.

Ask your coach: What should I do when I think I touched but no light appeared?

Useful words

Piste / strip 剑道
The marked area where the bout takes place.
Valid target 有效部位
The body area where a touch can count for this weapon.

Think, then check

Both saber scoring lights appear. What can you conclude from the lights alone?

  1. Both fencers must score.
  2. The first light always wins.
  3. The referee must consider the actions before awarding a touch.
  4. The exchange was necessarily simultaneous.

Answer: The referee must consider the actions before awarding a touch.

Two lights do not reveal priority by themselves. The referee judges the fencing sequence.

Move with Control

Build movement you can trust, one clear cue at a time.

Can you finish your step ready for your next choice?

Your feet help you manage distance and prepare your next choice. Review the movements your coach has already taught, and notice what helps you finish in control. Keep new techniques for coached practice.

Key ideas

  • Use your coach's stance and movement cues.
  • Notice the finish as well as the start.
  • Control matters more than racing.

Your mission: Watch an advance and a retreat. Name what moves first in each.

Foundations

Learn the order

In a basic advance, the front foot leads and the rear foot follows. In a basic retreat, the rear foot leads and the front foot follows. Review your coach's demonstration.

Return to ready

After a familiar step, check whether you can settle into your on-guard position without wobbling. Avoid reaching farther than your control allows.

Build Your Skills

Compare step sizes

Watch the same fencer use small and large steps. Ask which movement leaves more room to adjust before committing.

Include recovery

An action does not end with the picture of a lunge. Watch how the fencer recovers and prepares for what happens next.

Challenge

Spot commitment

Pause just before a fencer finishes an attack. Which choices are still available? Which would require a large change in movement?

Know the saber restriction

Do not copy a running fleche from another weapon. Forward crossing with the rear foot completely passing the front foot is forbidden in saber.

Ask your coach: What is the one movement cue you want me to remember?

Useful words

Advance 前进一步
A fencing step toward the opponent.
Retreat 后退一步
A fencing step away from the opponent.

Think, then check

During a basic retreat, which foot usually leads?

  1. The front foot.
  2. The rear foot.
  3. Both feet must jump.
  4. Either foot crosses over the other.

Answer: The rear foot.

The rear foot leads in a basic retreat, then the front foot follows. Use the exact movement your coach teaches.

Distance and Timing

Watch the gap, not only the blade.

How could changing the gap create your chance?

Distance changes whenever either fencer moves. The same attack can reach in one situation and miss in another. Look at the gap, the movement of both fencers, and the moment the attacker commits.

Key ideas

  • Both fencers change the distance.
  • Reach depends on the action and the opponent's movement.
  • Watch what happens after a miss.

Your mission: Predict whether an attack will reach, fall short, or get too close.

Foundations

Watch the gap

Play a short exchange once while watching only the distance. Does it shrink, grow, or stay similar before the final action?

Notice both movements

A retreat by one fencer and an advance by the other happen together. Do not judge distance from one person's feet alone.

Build Your Skills

See a pull-short

A defender may use distance to make an attack miss. Identify the actual finish and miss before describing the next action.

Find the change

Look for a small step becoming a longer finish, or a steady approach becoming a burst. Ask what the opponent did at that moment.

Challenge

Think beyond the miss

An avoided attack creates an opportunity, not a permanent possession of priority. Observe who makes the next valid action and whether it arrives.

Compare outcomes

Find two similar approaches: one reaches and one misses. Look for a difference in starting distance, step size, or the defender's response.

Ask your coach: What tells me that I am close enough to finish my attack?

Useful words

Distance 距离
The changing space between the fencers.
Tempo / rhythm 节奏
The pattern and timing of the actions.

Think, then check

One fencer's attack falls short. What should you watch next?

  1. Only the crowd.
  2. Assume the defender owns the rest of the bout.
  3. Which action follows, and whether it succeeds.
  4. Ignore everything after the miss.

Answer: Which action follows, and whether it succeeds.

The miss matters, but the following actions still determine the rest of the exchange.

Meet the Main Actions

Give the exchange a clear name.

Is every hit an attack?

Fencers can hit during different kinds of actions. Learning their names helps you describe what happened. Start with attack, parry, riposte, and counterattack before adding more detailed terms.

Key ideas

  • Attack starts an offensive action.
  • A parry defends with the weapon.
  • A riposte follows a parry; a counterattack happens into an attack.

Your mission: Name the actions in one short exchange without naming a winner yet.

Foundations

Tell a short story

Try 'attack, parry, riposte.' Saying the actions in order is more useful than saying 'they both swung.'

Compare two responses

A riposte comes after a parry. A counterattack is made during the opponent's offensive action. They are different decisions.

Build Your Skills

Preparation has a purpose

Preparation can help a fencer judge distance, draw a reaction, or set up an attack. Moving forward alone does not settle every priority question.

Simple and compound

A simple attack has one action. A compound attack includes feints. Count the threats you can actually see, not every small blade movement.

Challenge

Learn renewals gradually

Remise, redoublement, and reprise describe different renewed offensive actions. First identify that the original action ended; then ask your coach which name fits.

Name before judging

Separate three questions: What actions happened? Which touches registered? How do the rules apply? Mixing them too early can hide an important detail.

Ask your coach: Can you show me a riposte and a counterattack side by side?

Useful words

Parry 防守格挡
A defensive blade action that stops an offensive action arriving.
Riposte 还击
The offensive action after parrying.

Think, then check

A fencer blocks an attack with a successful parry, then hits back. What is the hit back called?

  1. A riposte.
  2. A salute.
  3. A preparation.
  4. Automatically a simultaneous attack.

Answer: A riposte.

A riposte is the offensive action made after a parry.

Priority: Who Gets the Touch?

Make your explanation clear, one action at a time.

Why can a fencer hit and still lose the touch?

Priority helps the referee decide between competing touches. Follow the whole exchange: how the attack began, whether it continued, whether it was defended, and what happened next.

Key ideas

  • A registered hit and an awarded touch are different.
  • A successful defense can change the sequence.
  • Judge the actions together, not one frozen pose.

Your mission: Explain a simple attack-versus-counterattack example in one sentence.

Foundations

Use a clear starting case

For two registered hits, compare a correctly executed simple attack with a counterattack that neither parries nor avoids it. The attack takes precedence.

Follow the defense

A successful parry gives the right to riposte. Watch for the immediate response; merely hearing blades touch does not establish a parry.

Build Your Skills

Find the turning point

Replay until you can point to the defense or the end of an unsuccessful attack. Then follow the new action, rather than assuming priority lasts forever.

Do not use shortcuts

The first foot movement, loudest shout, or first lamp you notice is not a complete judging method. The referee reads the fencing phrase.

Challenge

Respect uncertainty

One camera angle may hide the blade contact or timing. 'I cannot tell from this view' is a better answer than inventing a detail.

Connect rules to real calls

The written definition of attack and practical interpretation must be learned together. Ask your coach or a qualified referee to explain a difficult example.

Ask your coach: Which part of this exchange made you call attack, defense, or preparation?

Useful words

Priority / right of way 优先权
The conventions used to decide which action takes precedence.
Fencing phrase 交锋过程
The connected sequence of actions in an exchange.

Think, then check

A correctly executed simple attack hits. The opponent counterattacks, also hits, and neither parries nor avoids the attack. Who has priority?

  1. The counterattacker, because they also hit.
  2. The attacker.
  3. Both automatically score.
  4. Whichever fencer shouts first.

Answer: The attacker.

In this stated simple case, the attack takes precedence over the counterattack. The complete conditions matter.

Priority Detective

Look closely. Build an explanation you can support.

Does touching the other blade always win priority?

Some exchanges look almost alike but have different answers. Study one difference at a time. Preparation, simultaneous attacks, beats, delayed responses, and point-in-line each need their own conditions.

Key ideas

  • Two lights are not automatically simultaneous attacks.
  • Blade contact needs context.
  • Point-in-line is a specific position established in time.

Your mission: Identify one detail that changes the call between two examples.

Foundations

Simultaneous actions

If both fencers conceive and execute their attacks simultaneously, neither touch is awarded. Two lights alone do not prove that this happened.

Attack into preparation

Look for a valid attack made while the other fencer is still preparing. A single picture of a bent arm cannot prove the entire sequence.

Build Your Skills

Beat or parry?

Ask when contact occurred, what it did, and where on the blade it happened. The rules distinguish a beat on the foible from one on the forte.

After a pause

A hesitation after a parry may allow a renewed attack to take precedence. Avoid a made-up stopwatch rule; inspect the actual following actions.

Challenge

Point-in-line conditions

The line must exist before the attack starts and remain valid; the touch must arrive with the point. Raising a straight arm late is not enough.

Deflecting the line

An attacker facing an established line must first deflect it. A mere blade tap is insufficient. Watch whether the threatening point was really displaced.

Ask your coach: Can we compare a successful beat, a parry, and an incidental blade touch?

Useful words

Beat 击打剑身
A purposeful action against the opponent's blade.
Point-in-line 剑尖对准
A straight sword arm with the point continually threatening valid target.

Think, then check

Two lights appear. Which detail would support a simultaneous-attack decision?

  1. Both fencers conceived and executed their attacks simultaneously.
  2. Both fencers shouted loudly.
  3. One fencer touched the blade at some point.
  4. The scoring lights alone always prove it.

Answer: Both fencers conceived and executed their attacks simultaneously.

The simultaneous conception and execution of the attacks is the relevant condition. Two lights alone do not show the whole sequence.

Understand the Referee

Listen to the story of the touch.

Can you explain the call without looking at the score?

The referee uses commands, gestures, and words to manage the bout and explain decisions. Learn the meaning of the call first, then connect it to the official gesture chart.

Key ideas

  • Know start and stop commands.
  • Left and right depend on the referee's view.
  • Hear the actions before the final award.

Your mission: Listen to one call and repeat the actions in their order.

Foundations

Prepare, then start

Get ready when instructed, remain still, and wait for the start command. If you are not ready, tell the referee clearly before the start.

Stop and listen

On 'Halt,' stop and do not start a new action. Listen for words such as attack, parry, riposte, and touch.

Build Your Skills

Orient the picture

In a video, decide who is on the referee's left and right. A camera on the opposite side can make a familiar gesture confusing.

Use the official chart

Compare the call with the FIE referee-gesture chart in the reference section. Avoid learning invented poses from an unlabeled drawing.

Challenge

Ask a clear question

If you need an explanation, ask politely at the appropriate pause: 'Could you explain the action?' Listen without arguing through the next exchange.

Separate rules and judgment

Disagreeing with an observed action is different from a question about a rule's application. Let your coach help you understand the event's procedures.

Ask your coach: Which referee words and gestures should I learn first?

Useful words

En garde / on guard 准备姿势
The command to take the ready position.
Allez / fence / play 开始
Common language forms of the command to begin.

Think, then check

The referee says 'Halt.' What is your next job?

  1. Start one last attack.
  2. Stop and listen for the decision.
  3. Run back before hearing the call.
  4. Argue about the lights immediately.

Answer: Stop and listen for the decision.

Stop the exchange and listen. Do not begin a new action after the halt.

Build Your Attack Plan

Make a plan you can explain and adapt.

What will your approach ask your opponent to do?

Build an attack plan with an approach, something to notice, and a possible finish. Stay ready to adapt when the opponent changes the distance or their response. Understanding your choice gives you something useful to learn from every attempt.

Key ideas

  • Approach with a purpose.
  • Notice the opponent's response.
  • Finish with control, then be ready again.

Your mission: Describe an attack plan as 'If I see this, I will consider that.'

Foundations

Pick one clear idea

Before a supervised practice exchange, choose the simple action your coach assigned. Focus on doing it with control and recognizing its result.

Watch the response

Does the opponent retreat, prepare a parry, or come forward? Notice the response before deciding that your original plan must continue.

Build Your Skills

Compare approaches

Watch a short start-line action and a longer moving attack. Identify how each fencer manages distance before finishing.

Use a feint with purpose

A feint should ask the opponent a question. In a clip, name the reaction it appears to draw before the next action.

Challenge

Prepare a second option

Choose a simple alternative with your coach. For example, ask how your plan changes if the opponent retreats farther than expected.

Evaluate the decision

A successful touch does not prove every part of a plan was good. Ask what information supported the choice and whether the same situation may return.

Ask your coach: What response should I look for before finishing this attack?

Useful words

Direct attack 直接进攻
An attack that goes straight to its intended target line.
Feint 假动作威胁
A threat used to provoke a response before another action.

Think, then check

Your opponent retreats farther than you expected. Which is the best thinking habit?

  1. Stretch as far as possible regardless of balance.
  2. Assume your original finish must still reach.
  3. Reassess the distance and use a coached option.
  4. Ignore the opponent's movement.

Answer: Reassess the distance and use a coached option.

An attack plan needs to respond to the real distance. Review physical changes with your coach.

Make Defense Your Opportunity

Create a chance and be ready to use it.

What happens after you make the attack miss?

Defense gives you active choices. A fencer may avoid an attack with distance or stop it with a parry. Watch how the defender recognizes the result and chooses what comes next.

Key ideas

  • Distance defense and blade defense are different.
  • A parry must deal with the arriving attack.
  • Notice the next action after successful defense.

Your mission: Find one distance defense and one parry-riposte in a clip.

Foundations

Compare two solutions

A retreat can take the target out of reach. A parry uses the weapon to defend. Identify which solution you can actually see.

Do not guess from sound

A blade sound might be a beat, a parry, or incidental contact. Look for whether the defensive action stopped the attack's line.

Build Your Skills

Follow the riposte

When the parry succeeds, watch the immediate offensive response. How does the defender find a target without adding an unnecessary pause?

Remember the strip

A plan that needs another retreat must account for the rear limit. Watch how a fencer's choices change when space becomes smaller.

Challenge

Avoid hunting blindly

Notice whether a defender reacts to a real threat or reaches after a blade that moves away. Ask your coach how to make the parry compact and purposeful.

Review a failed defense

Name the problem precisely: the attack still reached, the parry did not close the line, or the next action was late. Avoid simply writing 'bad defense.'

Ask your coach: Which defensive choice fits the distance I usually face?

Useful words

Parry 3 / tierce 第三防守姿势
A commonly taught saber parry for the outside line.
Parry 4 / quarte 第四防守姿势
A commonly taught saber parry for the inside line.
Parry 5 / quinte 第五防守姿势
A commonly taught saber parry protecting the head line.

Think, then check

You hear blades touch during a clip. What do you know for certain?

  1. A successful parry happened.
  2. The defender automatically scores.
  3. There was contact; you still need to inspect its purpose and effect.
  4. The exchange must stop.

Answer: There was contact; you still need to inspect its purpose and effect.

Contact alone does not establish a parry. The action and its effect matter.

Read Your Opponent

Look for patterns, then test a small idea.

Which pattern could help you choose your next action?

An opponent gives you information through repeated choices. Notice patterns without assuming they will continue forever. Build a small hypothesis, choose a coached response, and see what happens.

Key ideas

  • Describe actions, not personalities.
  • A pattern is a clue, not a promise.
  • Change one idea at a time.

Your mission: Watch three exchanges and record one repeated behavior.

Foundations

Use neutral observations

Write 'retreated after my first step' rather than 'was scared.' The first statement tells you something you can watch again.

Count before concluding

One exchange can be unusual. Watch several before deciding that an action is a habit.

Build Your Skills

Connect pattern and option

If a clip shows a repeated parry, look for whether the attacker later changes the target or action. Describe the change before guessing why.

Test a small adjustment

Change one planned feature with your coach, such as approach rhythm. Then compare the opponent's response with what happened before.

Challenge

Think one response ahead

In second intention, the first action invites something. Ask: What response might the fencer want, and what evidence supports that idea?

Expect adaptation

Your opponent can notice patterns too. A successful plan may need adjustment once they respond differently. Keep observing after you score.

Ask your coach: What pattern did you notice in my opponent that I missed?

Useful words

Pattern 重复规律
An action or choice that appears more than once.
Second intention 第二意图
An opening action used to draw a response for a planned next action.

Think, then check

Which note is most useful for tactical learning?

  1. My opponent is unbeatable.
  2. They retreated after the first step in three exchanges.
  3. They looked mean.
  4. I need a completely new style.

Answer: They retreated after the first step in three exchanges.

A specific repeated action gives you something to investigate and discuss with your coach.

Learn from a Fencing Clip

Follow your curiosity and explain what you see.

Which decision would you like to understand?

Choose a fencer whose decisions interest you. Watch one thing at a time: start with the full exchange, replay a detail, and build your own explanation. Say when the camera does not show enough to decide.

Key ideas

  • Watch once at normal speed.
  • Choose a specific observation for the replay.
  • Separate observation, interpretation, and the official call.

Your mission: Replay one exchange and write 'I saw…' followed by 'I think…'.

Foundations

Choose a viewing job

Watch only the distance on the first replay. On the next, follow the blade action. Narrowing your job makes a fast exchange easier to follow.

Use a pause wisely

Pause before the finish and predict an option. Resume to check. Explain what matched your prediction and what surprised you.

Build Your Skills

Find the turning point

Mark the moment when the action changes: a miss, a parry, a new attack, or a visible adjustment in rhythm. Describe what you see.

Listen after the action

Hear the referee's call and compare it with your description. A commentator's guess and the official decision are not always the same thing.

Challenge

Know the camera's limits

Blur, a blocked blade, or a cut in the footage can hide decisive information. Say what additional view you would need.

Link the touches

Watch several exchanges in sequence. Does an action repeat? Does a fencer change something? Treat claims about intention as hypotheses, not facts.

Ask your coach: Can you help me interpret this specific exchange and camera view?

Useful words

Observation 观察到的事实
Something visible or audible in the footage.
Interpretation 解释或推测
Your explanation of what the actions might mean.

Think, then check

The blades are hidden behind a fencer at the decisive moment. What is the best response?

  1. Invent a parry so the story is complete.

A clear attack meets a counterattack

Foundations

  1. A: A begins a correctly executed simple attack and continues it without interruption.
  2. B: B tries to hit during A's attack, without parrying or avoiding it.
  3. Both: Both hit valid target during the same exchange.

Think, then check

Both valid lights appear. The complete sequence below is known, with no equipment problem or other fault. Who scores?

  1. A scores
  2. B scores because B also hits
  3. Both score
  4. Two lights always mean no touch

Answer: A scores

A's valid attack has priority over B's counterattack here. A second light does not by itself turn an exchange into simultaneous attacks.

Only B registers a valid hit. The referee confirms there was no fault, equipment problem, or reason to annul it. What happens?

  1. A scores because A began attacking
  2. B scores
  3. Neither can score unless both lights appear
  4. Both score

Answer: B scores

B's valid single hit scores. A light still needs the referee's decision: faults and equipment issues can change an apparent result.

Sources and references

  • FIE Technical Rules, August 2026: t.54, t.55, t.99, t.100

A new action after Halt

Foundations

  1. Both: The referee calls Halt and both fencers stop.
  2. A: A then starts a new attack and hits.
  3. B: B has remained stopped.

Think, then check

Both fencers have stopped. A begins a brand-new attack after the referee calls Halt and a light appears. Can that new action score?

  1. Yes, every light counts
  2. Yes, if it is the only light
  3. No; a new action started after Halt cannot score
  4. B must attack too

Answer: No; a new action started after Halt cannot score

Do not start a new action after Halt. The rules treat an action already begun before Halt differently; this scenario explicitly describes a new one.

Sources and references

  • FIE Technical Rules, August 2026: t.23.3, t.55.1

The parry is followed by a pause

Build Your Skills

  1. B: B successfully parries A's first attack.
  2. B: B then clearly hesitates without beginning a riposte.
  3. A: A renews during that pause.
  4. B: B starts a late response, without parrying the renewal. Both hit.

Think, then check

B parries but then pauses. During that clear pause A renews. B answers only after A's renewal begins, and both hit. Who scores?

  1. B keeps priority forever after a parry
  2. Both score
  3. No touch whenever a fencer pauses
  4. A scores

Answer: A scores

B's delay allows A's renewal in this specified sequence. There is no universal half-second shortcut; the referee follows the actions.

Sources and references

  • FIE Technical Rules, August 2026: t.105.1, t.106.3(c)

The distance detective

Foundations · Space

The camera sees a gap. You are looking for when that gap changes.

Your mission: Track one attack and one retreat

  1. Pick the fencer on the left at the start of an exchange.
  2. Watch both fencers’ feet before looking at the hits.
  3. Find an attack that reaches or falls short. What changed the gap?

Did the gap change before or during the finish? Describe what you saw.

A rhythm change

Build Your Skills · Timing

Similar-looking steps can create very different moments to attack.

Your mission: Compare two opening exchanges

  1. Choose two exchanges from the same bout.
  2. Compare step size and pauses during preparation.
  3. Look for a change of pace before the finish. Does the opponent react?

A change in rhythm can change when an opponent expects the finish. It is a tactical possibility, not proof of what a fencer intended. A quick finish alone does not establish priority.

What changed: step size, pace, distance, or more than one?

What comes after defense?

Foundations · Defense

Making an attack miss and parrying it are different defensive events.

Your mission: Follow the next action

  1. Find a clearly visible blade interception or an attack that falls short.
  2. Identify the first offensive action afterward.
  3. Listen to the referee’s complete phrase. Does it match your view?

A successful parry gives the right to riposte. An immediate simple riposte has priority over the attacker’s renewal under the relevant conditions. After a miss, identify who actually starts the next attack; priority is not a permanent possession.

Was there a parry, a miss, or too little evidence to decide?

The pattern and the adjustment

Build Your Skills · Strategy

One touch is an event. Several touches may reveal a pattern.

Your mission: Follow three exchanges

  1. Write one observation for each of three exchanges.
  2. Look for a repeated opening or defensive choice.
  3. Check whether either fencer changes their plan.

A useful strategy responds to evidence. Three examples are a small sample; treat a pattern as a working guess. Ask what option the other fencer could choose next.

Which observation supports your prediction? What would make you change it?

Feint or commitment?

Challenge · Preparation

Sometimes an action invites a response before the real finish.

Your mission: Separate observation from intention

  1. Look for a first threat followed by a changed finishing line.
  2. Notice whether the defender searches for a parry.
  3. If the blade or timing is hidden, keep your answer uncertain.

A feint threatens a target to draw a response. If a defender finds the blade during a compound attack, that can change the exchange. Without a clear view, you cannot reliably label every small hand motion a feint or preparation.

What is visible, and what are you inferring about the plan?

One team, changing situations

Challenge · Team strategy

In a team match, each relay begins with a score inherited from earlier relays.

Your mission: Compare the start and end of a relay

  1. Choose the team match in Classic matches.
  2. Record the team score when a relay begins and ends.
  3. Watch whether distance and opening choices change.

A team fencer faces an opponent and a changing team-score situation. Being ahead or behind may influence choices, but the score does not tell you the coach’s exact instructions.

Which choices changed, and which stayed the same?

Glossary

Advance 前进一步
A fencing step toward the opponent.
Allez / fence / play 开始
Common language forms of the command to begin.
Beat 击打剑身
A purposeful action against the opponent's blade.
Bout 一场比赛
A fencing contest between two fencers.
Cue 动作提示
A short reminder that helps you focus on one feature.
Direct attack 直接进攻
An attack that goes straight to its intended target line.
Direct elimination / DE 淘汰赛
A stage in which the winner advances in the bracket.
Distance 距离
The changing space between the fencers.
En garde / on guard 准备姿势
The command to take the ready position.
Evidence 依据
The detail that supports your explanation.
Feint 假动作威胁
A threat used to provoke a response before another action.
Fencing phrase 交锋过程
The connected sequence of actions in an exchange.
Foible / forte 剑身弱部 / 强部
Parts of the blade farther from / nearer to the guard.
Halt 停
The command that stops the fencing exchange.
Interpretation 解释或推测
Your explanation of what the actions might mean.
Lamé 金属衣
The conductive garment used for electrical scoring.
Mission 学习目标
One clear thing you want to notice or learn.
Observation 观察到的事实
Something visible or audible in the footage.
Parry 防守格挡
A defensive blade action that stops an offensive action arriving.
Parry 3 / tierce 第三防守姿势
A commonly taught saber parry for the outside line.
Parry 4 / quarte 第四防守姿势
A commonly taught saber parry for the inside line.
Parry 5 / quinte 第五防守姿势
A commonly taught saber parry protecting the head line.
Pattern 重复规律
An action or choice that appears more than once.
Piste / strip 剑道
The marked area where the bout takes place.
Point-in-line 剑尖对准
A straight sword arm with the point continually threatening valid target.
Pool 小组赛
A group in which fencers have preliminary bouts.
Priority / right of way 优先权
The conventions used to decide which action takes precedence.
Process goal 过程目标
A goal about an action or habit you can control.
Reflection 复盘
Thinking about what happened and what you learned.
Reset 调整状态
A short routine for returning attention to the next task.
Retreat 后退一步
A fencing step away from the opponent.
Riposte 还击
The offensive action after parrying.
Saber / sabre 佩剑
Two English spellings for this fencing weapon.
Salute 敬礼
The formal gesture of respect used in fencing.
Second intention 第二意图
An opening action used to draw a response for a planned next action.
Self-check 自我检查
A question used to see what you understand.
Tempo / rhythm 节奏
The pattern and timing of the actions.
Valid target 有效部位
The body area where a touch can count for this weapon.
